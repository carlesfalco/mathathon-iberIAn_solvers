# Reading the results without overclaiming

## Three kinds of evidence

1. **Proved:** a mathematical identity or a short calculation whose assumptions are shown. For example, the bit-reversed DFT's complementary residue-class blocks are exactly rank one.
2. **Measured:** a numerical result for a particular problem, tolerance, implementation and reference. For example, the six to fifteen percent storage reduction in one Rust operator is not a universal cost law.
3. **Cited / plausible:** a known theorem or a reasonable mechanism not yet proved here. Prior-art proximity and novelty hypotheses must not be turned into "first-ever" claims from a failed search.

## Dense, fast, and exact answers to different questions

- **Dense reference:** a direct calculation used to check the compressed implementation. The `ExactCompressor` reads the real far blocks and is an oracle, not always a practical fast constructor.
- **Fast versus dense:** the consistency of their outputs at requested tolerance. This is not a speed ratio.
- **Analytic/invariant error:** comparison with a closed-form solution, formula or identity. This may be dominated by discretization rather than compression.
- **Recorded time:** useful measured performance at the stated size, but not the same thing as a flop model or an asymptotic guarantee.

Always check whether “error” means forward error, residual/backward error, discretization error, or error in a functional such as drag, log determinant or posterior mean.

## Result files

- `unified/showcase/results.json` and `RESULTS.md`: ten model checks, dials, parameters, code-line counts and caveats. The current file was generated by the project's full showcase run on an arm64 laptop.
- `unified/explore/*/results/*.json`: direct experimental outputs. These are the authoritative inputs for checking a numerical claim.
- `unified/explore/*/results/*.png`: visualization of the same results. Inspect the associated text before treating a figure as a physics result.
- `../../results/verification/`: independently rerun DFT, E1 and E12 checks, with commands and output files.
- `../../results/update-review.md`: consolidated E6–E12 findings, which claims do not survive, and research priorities.

## Results worth understanding

### A precise algebraic result

For the bit-reversed DFT, the residue-class relation makes every complementary tile rank one. The generic ID butterfly then has exactly $2N\log_2N+2N$ stored entries in the checked construction. This is a proof about those orderings and that factor representation, not a statement that all FFT algorithms allocate this storage.

### The shared interface transfers, with local changes

Ten showcase models passed quick agreement checks. A shared core and drivers are genuinely reused. One example uses the E5 solver fork, and six need sampling-layer changes. “Core unchanged” should mean the core files are unchanged, not that every new physics problem is literally zero code.

### Accuracy is not speed

In the recorded full showcase, four fast paths beat dense timings, five lost, and one approximately tied. The underlying drivers retain dense working matrices, so absolute timing slopes do not prove a production sparse asymptotic.

### Cost can increase when ranks fall

- E6 Stokes uses roughly $2\times$ per-box skeletons versus Laplace, about $1\times$ per dof, and about $4\times$ storage at equal geometry.
- E12 Oseen directional admissibility gives a roughly threefold root reduction in the tested high-$\lambda$ cases. At $N=4096$, it instead yields $21.6\%$ fewer skeletons but $28.6\%$ more storage and $67.3\%$ more elimination flops. That is not a total-cost win.
- E10's storage savings are measured per spherical-harmonic order. They do not compose into an all-order transform storage/time win.

### Better sampling changes a span, not just a point count

E8 shows that refining a flat proxy circle does not fix its representation error, whereas spreading points over latitude bands or an annulus can work. E11 shows the corresponding multi-order Matérn idea and that a simple ring with an extra kernel order can be cheaper than a lifted proxy. Exact local-lift arguments cover integer-codimension cases; fractional cases are numerical evidence unless further analysis is supplied.

### A precise solve does not guarantee precise physics

E6 near contact, E7 screened-layer quadrature and E9 volume discretization can have accurate fast-versus-dense solves while physical/discretization errors remain large. In E12, the drag functional can be accurate despite a large raw density error, because matrix and functional conditioning are different.

### Complex elimination requires the right algebra

E5 identifies the ordinary-versus-conjugate transpose issue. The adjoint congruence has an exact positivity proof under the stated Hermitian-part hypothesis; practical error amplification is regime dependent. E9 requires scoped recommendations, not a blanket realification prescription.

## What not to quote from these files alone

- A universal $\mathrm O(N\log N)$ solve/build theorem from finite-$N$ dense working-array timings.
- A universal rank formula or cost comparison using different norms, tolerances or SVD-vs-QR counts.
- A full fractional-lift proof, a complete Rust port or a universal solver.
- A universal “rank $2\times$” or “max rank plus one” claim for stacked IDs; the safe upper bound is $\operatorname{rank}[A;B]\leq\operatorname{rank}A+\operatorname{rank}B$, with overlap measured separately.
- Publication-quality novelty. The relevant literature and the negative searches are documented in the audits; absence found in a search is not proof of absence.
